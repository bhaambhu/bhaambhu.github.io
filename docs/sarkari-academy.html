Sarkari Academy
Millions of people in India prepare for government jobs, and the notices that announce them are scattered across hundreds of recruiting bodies, each with its own website, PDF format and update schedule. Sarkari Academy brings them into one place, turns each notice into structured data, and tells a candidate which jobs they are actually eligible for, from their qualifications, date of birth, category and other details.
It is a solo project, live at sarkariacademy.com since September 2026, with an Android app in Google Play testing.

Architecture
One Laravel backend on PostgreSQL serves three kinds of client, and all three call the same service classes. Business logic lives in services, never in controllers, so the website, the staff tools and the app can never disagree about a job.
>
Public site and signed-in users: Inertia.js with React and TypeScript, styled with Tailwind and shadcn/ui.
>
Staff panel: a notice editor for positions, category-wise vacancies, qualifications with AND/OR rules, fees, age limits and a timeline of updates.
>
Mobile API: token-authenticated JSON endpoints (Laravel Sanctum) for the Expo app, thin wrappers over the same services.
>
Deployed with Coolify behind Cloudflare, errors in Sentry, email through Postmark and Brevo, push notifications through Firebase Cloud Messaging.
Eligibility matching
A notice stores each post's qualifications as a small rule tree: groups that must all be satisfied, each holding alternatives where any one will do, with conditions such as subject, minimum marks, relaxed marks for reserved categories and years of experience. A candidate's profile is matched against those rules, their age on the notice's cut-off date, their category's relaxations and any quota a post is restricted to. The result is a personal feed of the jobs they qualify for, ordered by how soon they close.
Android app
The app is built with Expo and React Native in TypeScript, with Google sign-in, onboarding that builds the eligibility profile, a personal "For You" feed, follow and push alerts, and full notice pages.
>
Release channels: internal preview builds and Play closed-testing builds take over-the-air JavaScript updates on their own channels, published by one script that tests, type-checks, versions and ships.
>
Store builds take no OTA updates. Instead the app asks the backend which release is current. Admins manage releases, "What's new" notes and a minimum supported build from the staff panel, and builds below the minimum show a blocking update screen.
>
Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, with a privacy policy and in-app account deletion to meet Play's requirements.
What's next
The long-term goal is preparation, not just alerts: multiple-choice practice organised into topics, topics combined into each job's syllabus, and a measure of how prepared a candidate is for every job they are eligible for. This builds on the adaptive-learning ideas from my research and from CAPTAIN.
